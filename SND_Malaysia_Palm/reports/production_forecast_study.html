Malaysian CPO production by state, Oct 2026 – Dec 2027

I forecast each of the 12 producing states separately and add them up to get Malaysia. The model is a weather-lag regression fitted across all states together, multiplied by a state El Niño factor fitted on 40 years of MPOB yields. These numbers come from the data, not from the house forecast.

Final state-up number: 2027 = 18.40 Mt, −10.0% on 2026. Base effects account for −2.1% and El Niño for −8.0%. The range is 18.07–18.78 Mt across moderate to very strong El Niño, with about ±4 points of model error on top. Q4 2026 still looks seasonally normal. The surge in September came from timing and dry harvesting weather, not from a bigger crop. In 2027 the strong El Niño now underway cuts output by about 10%, mostly between May and December, when the 8–11 month flower-abortion lag bites. Every state falls. The largest drops come where 2026 ran hottest (Sabah, Sarawak, Johor, Perak). States that had a weak 2026 (Pahang, Kelantan, Kedah) fall less, because they start from a lower base.

01 · Context

Why September and October 2026 are so strong

  1. Mostly timing. Over July–September 2026, output was only +4.0% above a year earlier (August and September together, +6.5%). September alone was +15%, so about two-thirds of that jump is crop moved between months. The Peninsula had 25 working days in September 2026 against 22 in September 2025, which had three public-holiday losses (+14% harvest days). It had 24 against 26 in August, so crop that missed August was cut in September. Sabah and Sarawak had the same 26 days both years, so their jump is not a calendar effect.
  2. Dry, sunny weather helps harvesting in the short run. August rainfall was 56–78% below normal in every major state, and September 16–43% below. Fewer rain-lost days, passable field roads and faster ripening pulled crop forward into September. Oil extraction rose to 20.26% from 19.73% (+2.7% more oil per tonne of fruit); Sabah reached 21.25%. Fruit yield was 14–20% above a year earlier in Johor, Perak, Sabah, Sarawak and Terengganu.
  3. The bunches being cut now were set before the drought. The flowers behind today's crop passed their abortion window 8–11 months ago (late 2025 to early 2026), when RONI was near zero. Sex differentiation (about two years back) happened in 2024, a wet year (112–135% of normal rainfall). The palms are carrying a full crop formed in good conditions.
  4. Weak base last year. September 2025 was soft (−0.7% on August), and 2025's peak arrived in October (+11%). That flatters September 2026 and makes October harder to beat. The model has October 2026 at only +1% on a year earlier.
  5. This is the El Niño pattern, not evidence against it. In 2015, the drought year itself, output ran +9% to +12% above the year before through mid-year. 2016 then fell 13%. Strong output now fits a sharp drop 8–11 months later. Crop pulled into September also means a steeper fall into November and December.
02 · States

Final numbers, built state by state

Each state's 2027 change splits into two parts. The base effect is what its own monthly model says with no El Niño: momentum, the reversal of an unusually strong or weak 2026, and harvest calendar. The El Niño effect is the multiplier from the 40-year model. Malaysia is the sum of the 12 states.

Should drier states take a bigger El Niño hit?

I tested whether the hit should be shared out by each state's own drought exposure. That exposure is rainfall shortfall over the 8–11 month window: observed for Feb–Oct 2026, and the same state's Nov–Apr rainfall in 1997/98 and 2015/16 for the months still to come. In the two big past events, a state's exposure did not predict its yield loss (correlation +0.01 in 1998, −0.10 in 2016). Losses were broad-based, probably because one weather point per state is coarse and haze, sunlight and heat hit every state. So the final numbers keep a near-uniform El Niño sensitivity. The exposure-tilted result is shown only as a sensitivity. Nationally, though, rainfall exposure for the 2027 harvest is already higher than before 2016 or 1998, which supports the strong scenario.

Record heat in 2026: what it adds

2026 is the hottest year in the 1990–2026 ERA5 record for Malaysia's oil palm states, by a wide margin. I tested whether heat hurts yields beyond what El Niño already explains.

  • Heat partly comes with El Niño. Each +1 °C of RONI brings about +0.4 °C of Malaysian heat, plus a warming trend of +0.15 °C per decade. After removing both, 2026's extra heat is +0.78 °C. That's the largest on record, but close to 1998 (+0.74 °C).
  • In the 40-year state panel, heat adds no clean effect of its own. Alone it shows zero. Beside El Niño, adjacent lag windows take opposite signs, which points to overlapping variables rather than a real effect. The past hot years were the El Niño years, so their heat damage already sits inside the El Niño coefficient.

  • Conclusion: the central number stays at 18.40 Mt. The heat is still a reason to lean towards the downside. 2026 is outside the historical range, heat above about 33–35 °C hurts pollination and fruit set, and the model already under-predicted 2016 (a hot, hazy year) by about 12 points. Treat the very-strong case (−11%) and a 2016-type outcome (−13% to −15%) as the realistic downside.

04 · Study

How the model works

Step 1 · Monthly regression, 12 states pooled

ln P(state,T) − ln P(state,T−12) = b0 + b1·workdays + b2·base_dev + b3·mom3 + b4·mom12 + b5·origin_dev + b6·dry(6–12m lag) + b7·dry(19–30m lag) One ridge fit per horizon (1–15 months), pooled across 12 states (2010–2026); each state uses its own rainfall and calendar. base_dev = base month vs its seasonal norm (base effects reverse); origin_dev = latest actual vs its norm.

Step 2 · Why the monthly model can't size a drought

The 2010–26 weather file contains only one drought-then-crash (2016), and it barely registers the 2015 Sabah drought. The monthly fit explains −1% to −3% of 2016's actual −16% to −17%. Its drought effect is therefore shrunk towards zero, and it can't see an El Niño forecast.

Step 3 · When dryness shows up in output

Correlation between production growth (vs a year earlier) and the change in a 3-month rainfall-deficit measure L months earlier. The peaks fall at 2–3 months (oil filling) and 8–11 months (flower abortion), with a rebound at 13–16 months and a weaker effect at 24–28 months (sex ratio).

Step 4 · Forty years of El Niños

I fitted MPOB regional CPO production per mature hectare (1986–2025, 120 region-years) against NOAA's RONI index. Each +1 °C of El Niño strength over the 8–11 month window cuts yield by 7.6% (t = -4.0). The 24–28 month term is not significant. The state estate-yield panel (n = 384) gives a steeper −0.110 (t = -10.6). The build uses it only to rank states, and rescales it to the production figure.

Step 5 · Combining them

  • Oct–Dec 2026: monthly model as is. The El Niño window for these months (Nov 2025–Apr 2026) had RONI ≈ 0, so there is no El Niño effect yet.
  • 2027: each state's monthly model with its dry-month effect removed (the same drought channel), multiplied by exp(βstate × El Niño strength 8–11 months earlier). The cut grows from 1% in January to 13–14% in July–October 2027.
  • RONI path: follows NOAA's 8 Oct outlook (strong-to-very-strong through Jan–Mar 2027, neutral from Apr–Jun 2027). Scenarios peak at 1.5 (moderate), 2.0 (strong) and 2.5 °C (very strong). The September reading was 1.69.
  • Cross-check: the 40-year annual model on its own gives 18.9 Mt for 2027. The state sum gives 18.4 Mt. The gap comes from momentum and base effects in the monthly model.
Limits. El Niño explains about a fifth of year-to-year yield variation. A record-strength event would be outside the sample. The model gives every state nearly the same El Niño sensitivity, because the data can't separate them. Each state's weather comes from a single point. Temperature (ERA5 1990–2026, saved to data/era5_state_temperature_1990_2026.json) was tested and left out of the forecast, because it adds nothing robust beyond El Niño; see section 02. Sarawak's 2022–24 rainfall readings are implausibly high, which the dry-month measure tolerates. Mature area is not modelled separately in the monthly route.
