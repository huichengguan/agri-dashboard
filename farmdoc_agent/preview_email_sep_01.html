⚡ Executive Trade Synthesis

  • Core Market Sentiment: Bullish Soybeans & Vegoil / Rangebound Corn / Geopolitically Supported Wheat
  • Primary Catalyst:
  • EPA SRE Reallocation Decision: The EPA ruled on 34 Small Refinery Exemption (SRE) petitions for 2025 (waiving ~1.8B gallons), but announced a supplemental rulemaking for late October to reallocate 100% of these waived volumes into 2026/2027 RFS obligations (up from the expected 70%). This makes the release a fundamental net-wash in total volume and provides a structural tailwind for Biomass-Based Diesel (BBD) and Soybean Oil.
  • Soybean Technical Breakout & Export Demand: November Soybeans broke above key $13.00 technical resistance driven by fresh fund inflows, robust domestic crush margins, and a flash export sale of 136,000 MT to China.
  • Geopolitical Supply Chain Friction: Failed Black Sea peace talks via Turkey, fresh missile attacks on Black Sea shipping, and Iranian sea-lane aggression near the Strait of Hormuz provided an active geopolitical risk floor for wheat and energy-linked commodities.
  • Vegoil & Energy / Cross-Commodity Spillover: The 100% RFS reallocation of SRE waivers is directly bullish for Soybean Oil, Biodiesel, and Renewable Diesel (RD), locking in future mandated demand. Ethanol/Corn remain insulated and unaffected by the SRE ruling.

🏛️ Fundamentals, Policy & Macro Catalysts

  • Biofuel & Policy Mandates:
  • EPA granted 34 SRE petitions for 2025 (~1.8B gallons waived across 100% and 50% waiver classifications).
  • Institutional Takeaway: Scott Irwin notes that the EPA's commitment to reallocate 100% of waived volumes into 2026–2027 mandates completely neutralizes the headline demand loss, removing a major overhang and solidifying medium-term biofuel demand.
  • Physical Demand & Export Pace:
  • USDA confirmed a flash sale of 136,000 MT of soybeans for delivery to China.
  • Geopolitical focus centers on the Shanghai Cooperation Organization (SCO) summit in Kyrgyzstan (attended by China, Russia, India, Iran, Pakistan), underscoring shifting trade alliances and alternative commodity sourcing corridors.
  • Weather & Crop Progress:
  • US Corn Belt: High heat pattern across the South, West, and Central Corn Belt projected to persist for 7–10 days before easing mid-month. The I-80 corridor will see localized thunderstorm relief, but MO, KS, OK, and SE NE remain severely hot and dry.
  • South America: Mato Grosso soybean planting window officially opens. While winter moisture was confined to Argentina and Southern Brazil, weather models project 1–2 inches of rain across Central/Northern Brazil over the next 7–10 days, setting up timely initial field work.
  • Input Costs & Fertilizer:
  • Phosphate (DAP/MAP) Shock: Nick Paulson highlights that DAP/MAP prices have steadily climbed over two years, with a sharp acceleration since March driven by skyrocketing Elemental Sulfur costs.
  • Sulfur—a vital input for phosphate production (analogous to natural gas for anhydrous ammonia)—has surged from under $100/ton in early 2024 to $300–$400/ton, and recently spiked above $1,000/ton globally.
  • Primary causes: Global petroleum refining disruptions, infrastructure war damage, and Strait of Hormuz transit risks. High sulfur pricing will keep DAP/MAP costs structurally elevated for the fall application window.
