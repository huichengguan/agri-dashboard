Empirical evaluation of NOAA CPC Relative Oceanic Niño Index (RONI) and Traditional Oceanic Niño Index (ONI) against ECMWF actualized rainfall across Overall Kalimantan and its 5 provinces (West, Central, South, East, and North Kalimantan) from Jan 2010 to Sep 2026 (n = 200 overlapping 3-month seasons).

1. Is There A Correlation?
Yes — Strong Inverse
Full-year Overall Kalimantan rainfall anomaly correlates with RONI at r = −0.484 (p = 4.7×10−13). During the Jun–Oct Dry Season, correlation surges to r = −0.702 (Spearman ρ = −0.752, p = 1.4×10−13).
2. RONI vs. Traditional ONI
RONI Outperforms (+28%)
NOAA's RONI beats legacy ONI in every Kalimantan region (Full-Year Overall r = −0.484 vs. −0.379; Dry Season r = −0.702 vs. −0.615) by removing background tropical warming bias.
3. Does Lagging Rainfall Help?
Yes: +1 to +2 Mo (Region/Season)
North Kalimantan peaks at Lag +1 to +2 mos year-round (r = −0.553) and Lag +3 mos in Nov–Apr (r = −0.601). In the Jun–Oct Dry Season, Central, South & West Kalimantan peak at Lag +1 mo (r = −0.649) and remain strong at Lag +2 mos (r = −0.632).
4. Regional Vulnerability Gradient
East & South Most Exposed
During El Niño (RONI ≥ +0.5°C) dry seasons, rainfall drops −52.8% in South, −49.5% in East, and −42.2% in Central Kalimantan, versus −21.9% in West Kalimantan.

5. Detailed Analytical Findings: Correlation & Lag Behavior in Kalimantan

Key takeaways answering your research questions on correlation strength, regional heterogeneity, and rainfall lag dynamics.

1. Why RONI Beats Traditional ONI Across All 5 Kalimantan Provinces

Over 2010–2026, background global tropical ocean warming has shifted raw Niño 3.4 SST anomalies upward. NOAA CPC's Relative Oceanic Niño Index (RONI) subtracts the 20°N–20°S tropical mean SST anomaly, isolating the true zonal sea-surface temperature gradient that drives Walker Circulation subsidence over Borneo. Consequently, RONI improves the full-year Overall Kalimantan correlation from r = −0.379 (ONI) to r = −0.484 (RONI), and improves the Dry Season Oil-Palm Weighted correlation from r = −0.615 (ONI) to r = −0.702 (RONI, Spearman ρ = −0.752).

2. Does Lagging Rainfall Give a Better Correlation? (The Two Regimes)

(A) During the Critical Dry Season (Jun–Oct): YES, Lag +1 to +2 Months improves correlation in Kalimantan's core plantation belt. Lagging rainfall by +1 month (using preceding month's RONI to predict current month's deseasonalized rainfall anomaly) improves correlation in Central Kalimantan from r = −0.621 to r = −0.642 (ρ = −0.684), in South Kalimantan from r = −0.635 to r = −0.649 (ρ = −0.707), and in West Kalimantan from r = −0.500 to r = −0.505 (ρ = −0.537). Even at Lag +2 months, Central and South Kalimantan maintain r = −0.632 and −0.629, offering a 1-to-2 month early warning window.
(B) North Kalimantan (ID-KU) Year-Round & Wet Season: YES, Lag +1 to +3 Months is strongest. Located at 3°N–4°N near Sabah, North Kalimantan's rainfall deficit peaks in Jan–Apr after the winter ENSO peak, so full-year correlation peaks at Lag +2 months (r = −0.553) and Wet-Season (Nov–Apr) correlation peaks at Lag +3 months (r = −0.601).

3. Why Full-Year Unconditioned Southern Kalimantan Peaks at Lag 0 / −1

When all 12 calendar months are pooled together without seasonal conditioning, West, Central, and South Kalimantan show their strongest correlation at Lag 0 (or Lag −1 to −2 months). Physically, this occurs because Southern Borneo's ENSO drought is seasonally phase-locked to July–October. By November–December, the Intertropical Convergence Zone (ITCZ) migrates southward across the Java Sea, restoring heavy monsoon rains to West and Central Kalimantan even while Pacific Niño 3.4 SSTs continue rising to their December–January (NDJ) peak. Thus, Kalimantan's dry-season rainfall trough occurs in Aug–Oct, roughly 1–2 months ahead of the December SST peak.

4. Strong East-to-West Geographic Gradient Across Kalimantan

Kalimantan is not meteorologically uniform under ENSO:
• East Kalimantan (ID-KI) & South Kalimantan (ID-KS) sit directly beneath the Makassar Strait branch of the Indonesian Throughflow and Pacific Walker subsidence cell, suffering −49.5% and −52.8% dry-season rainfall deficits during El Niño.
• Central Kalimantan (ID-KT) is buffered during the Nov–Apr wet monsoon (r = +0.17) but severely hit during the Jun–Oct dry monsoon (r = −0.688 at Lag +1m; −42.2% El Niño deficit).
• West Kalimantan (ID-KB) is weak on an unconditioned full-year basis (r = −0.211) due to South China Sea and Indian Ocean moisture, but becomes strongly correlated during the Jun–Oct Dry Season (r = −0.593, ρ = −0.656).
