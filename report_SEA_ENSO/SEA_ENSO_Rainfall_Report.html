5. Multi-Decadal Meteorological Conclusions: 1990–2026 Verification

Comparing the 37-year multi-decadal record against the 2010–2026 subset.

1. Multi-Decadal Confirmation: Indonesia's Extreme Sensitivity

Extending the record to 1990 confirms beyond doubt that Indonesia's dry-season drought engine is permanent and robust across 37 years:
• Overall Indonesia (1990–2026, n = 184 dry seasons): r = −0.821 (p = 4.6 × 10−22), almost identical to the 2010–2026 subset (r = −0.829).
• Capturing the 1997–1998 Super El Niño confirms the catastrophic vulnerability of South Sumatra, Bangka Belitung, and Kalimantan, where rainfall plunged by −60% to −75% below the 1991–2020 normal.

2. Malaysia's 37-Year Full Picture: Overall Correlation Emerges

When scaling back to 1990, Overall Malaysia's full-year correlation strengthens to r = −0.387 (p < 10−15) compared to r = −0.205 in the shorter 2010–2026 window:
• The 1997–98 event demonstrated that while Peninsular Malaysia can buffer moderate El Niños, extreme super El Niños break through the Malacca moisture trap, dropping dry-season rainfall by −28.5%.
• Northeast Monsoon (Nov–Apr) vulnerability remains Malaysia's dominant vulnerability across all 37 years (Overall Malaysia wet-season r = −0.434; Sabah r = −0.597; Perlis r = −0.549; Kedah r = −0.466; Pahang r = −0.466).

3. Indonesian Regional Vulnerability Hierarchy (37-Year Climatology)

Across 1990–2026, the dry-season ranking remains rock-solid:
1. Southwest Papua (r = −0.777) & North Sulawesi (r = −0.745).
2. Bangka Belitung (r = −0.743) & Southeast Sulawesi (r = −0.741).
3. South Kalimantan (r = −0.729) & East Kalimantan (r = −0.722).
4. Central Kalimantan (r = −0.692) & South Sumatra (r = −0.670).
5. North/West Sumatra (r = −0.15 to −0.29) remain largely decoupled from Pacific ENSO due to Indian Ocean Dipole (IOD) dominance.

4. Multi-Decadal Lag Recommendations

The 37-year record confirms the operational predictive windows:
• Southern Indonesia (Sumatera & Kalimantan Core Belts): Rainfall is strongly synchronized at Lag 0 to +1 month (r = −0.62 to −0.74).
• Northern Tropical Belts (Sabah, North Kalimantan, Northern Peninsular): Exhibit a +1 to +3 month lag following the winter solstice (Nov–Apr), giving plantation operators a reliable 60-to-90 day forward warning window to prepare soil conservation and irrigation protocols.
