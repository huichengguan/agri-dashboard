The 3 Gamma Regimes Explained

1. Dealer GEX (Standard Model) Assumes market makers are short calls and long puts against retail. Positive gamma dampens volatility (dealers buy dips, sell rallies); negative gamma accelerates trending moves.
2. Commercial Producer Collar Grain elevators and farmers buy put floors and sell upside call collars. This inverts dealer inventory to Short Puts & Long Calls, causing downward price dips to accelerate when dealers sell futures into falling markets.
3. Gross Stickiness Ignores directional long/short assumptions. Pure non-directional gamma thickness shows where options open interest creates the strongest frictional resistance and magnetic pinning into expiration.

VRP Bandwidth & Valuation Rules

Volatility Risk Premium: \(\text{VRP} = \text{ATM IV} - \text{30D Realized Vol}\). Indicates whether options are priced above or below actual physical grain price movement.

  • Near Channel Ceiling (> 70th %ile): Options are statistically expensive. Implied vol overprices physical movement; favor selling call collars, covered calls, and iron condors.
  • Near Channel Floor (< 30th %ile): Options are historically discounted. Physical swings exceed implied premiums; favor buying straddles, cheap put floors, or breakout calls.
  • Fair Value (30th - 70th %ile): Normal market equilibrium.
